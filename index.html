GRENDEL
Apocalyptic adventures in a fantasy Iron Age

Grendel is a narrative-driven, team-based roleplaying game where players take the role of Queen Sigrid’s Huscarls, standing resolute against the horrors of a new age. The game takes place in a pseudohistorical setting evocative of the Early Germanic Iron Age of northern Europe in the 4-6th Centuries CE, inspired by Beowulf and other epic tales of heroism against supernatural and horrific foes.

This world however was stricken some decade prior by a cosmic, spiritual malaise known as The Cold. The onset of this affliction has caused the soil to be stricken with salt, the rivers to turn sour, fruit to wither on the vine and the winters to become longer and longer. Even more alarming, beasts and spirits which were once the things of folklore and children’s tales now walk the land, and fleets of creatures dubbed ‘trolls’ raid the coastline. As far as Queen Sigrid’s people know they are the only peoples of the world who have not fallen victim to the Cold, the lone beacon of hope for humanity, however the noose grows tighter around their neck every day.

The tone of Grendel is one of grim survival, scarcity, and defiance against overwhelming, hopeless odds. Queen Sigrid’s people cannot triumph, yet fight on in the face of the inevitable. It is up to the players to be at the vanguard of that fight.

Role

The party in Grendel represents a team of Huscarls assembled by Queen Sigrid to meet some military, political or logistical objective. Each party is under the command of a lesser noble known as a Thegn, who is played by one of the players, and each other player takes a role in that Thegn’s retinue.

Your character represents a Thegn, Huscarl or aspiring Huscarl who has pledged themselves to the service of the Queen, to the Realm, and to humanity. Perhaps they bring a stout axe, a silver tongue, knowledge of lore and custom, expertise in hunting and survivalism, the gift of prophetic insight, or perhaps they can harness the calamity of this new age by speaking the ancient and powerful language of runes?

5) Select your traits

In addition to any traits gained earlier, your character will have various other qualities that are central to their identity. Choose traits as directed below:

(Note that unlike skills and knowledges, traits which are newly gained begin at rank 1.)

Q1) Which virtue do you embody?

All Huscarls and aspiring Huscarls have some outstanding personal quality which is highly valued by the community. Choose one trait from the list below:

Frugal; Heroic; Honourable; Hospitable; Industrious; Resolute; Selfless; Truthspeaker; Wise

No member of the party may embody the same virtue, so this question should be answered collaboratively. When making replacement or additional characters for existing parties, try to choose a trait which distinguishes you from the other party members.

Q2) What is your outlook towards the world and others?

How do you engage with your community, family and fellow Huscarls? What is your day-to-day attitude and approach to life?

Gain one trait from the list below:

Charming; Driven; Grim; Hopeful; Fierce; Independent; Keen-eyed; Stoic; Stubborn; Trickster; Warm-hearted; Wary

Q3) What is your defining characteristic?

Every character has something memorable which makes them stand out from their peers. Choose one trait you possess and increase its rank by one (up to a maximum of 3).

Q4) How has the world wearied you?

More experienced characters are likely to have suffered setbacks, losses, grief and hardship throughout the years.

Veterans and Advisors choose one from below: Doomed; Grey-haired; Lamenting; Oathsworn; Scarred; Shamed

Once you have selected your traits, note them on your character sheet. If you selected the same trait multiple times throughout the character creation process you may have a trait with a starting value of two or three. If so note this on your character sheet. The maximum rank for a trait is 3.

7) Final touches

Finally, you should take a moment to complete the remaining fields on your character sheet.

1. Name

Characters in Grendel tend to have names similar to those of Old Germanic, Anglo-Saxon and ancient Scandinavian languages and peoples.

2. Birth Season

Note whether your character was born in summer, autumn, winter or spring.

3. Adversary

If your character has an enemy, rival or other antagonist in their life you should note them here. This may be an old foe from before the coming of the darkness, another Huscarl you have a rivalry with, or anyone else (including another character you’ve previously noted on your character sheet, but not a party member).

If your character doesn’t have an adversary then you can leave this field blank. They may develop one throughout the campaign!

4. Appearance

Make a few notes about your character’s appearance. This may include body type, hair style and colour, and any distinguishing features.

5. Belief

Your belief is your character’s core value or ideal that is at the heart of their career as a Huscarl. This might be noble or self-serving. Some example beliefs are:

  • “I’d never ask my troops to do something I wouldn’t do myself”
  • “The community is more important than the individual”
  • “Whatever happens, I’ll make sure that I survive”
  • “Always be the first into battle and the last out”
  • “I would lay down my life for the Queen and the people”
  • “Although this world is ruined, I’ll still end up on top”

6. Mission and Goal

Your Mission is the task that you currently need to complete to fulfill your duties as a Huscarl. Your Goal is your character’s own personal objective. Leave both of these fields blank. The GM will present your party with a Mission during the first session, and afterwards you can note your character’s goal.
